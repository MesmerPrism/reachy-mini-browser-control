cache age 19.72 ms. All qualification children exited; no full backup or operating-

## Question and first candidate

The [matched P300/P360/P300 comparison](P_GAIN_TRANSACTION.md#october-19-completed-common-start-and-matched-p300p360p300)
showed a reversible reduction in motor-15 held error at identical delivered and
read-back goals. It did not remove all upward tilt. Several other joints also
lagged. The robot's physical assembly is to remain unchanged.

The next question is whether a small amount of integral action reduces motor
15's persistent following error without introducing overshoot, drift or unstable
holding. The first candidate changes **motor 15 only, I0 → I1 → I0**, with its
P300, D, feedforward, profiles, modes and protected limits retained exactly.
This isolates the response of one selected joint. It is a conservative mechanism
probe, not an expected whole-head leveling solution or a gain recommendation.

[ROBOTIS documents Position I Gain](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xl330-m288/#position-pid-gain80-82-84-feedforward-1st2nd-gains88-90)
at RAM register 82, two bytes, with internal coefficient equal to the raw value
divided by 65,536. I1 is the smallest nonzero register setting. It is not a
qualified safe setting merely because its number is small. Integral action
accumulates persistent error; a long hold can matter. The documented register
encoding range is not a Reachy tuning envelope. No I value above 1 is admitted.

The manual describes internal anti-windup that users cannot adjust. This trial
does not expose or validate its hidden state, reset behavior or effectiveness.
Reading I0 back proves register restoration, not that an internal accumulator
was cleared. Post-restoration motion and holding must be observed separately.

## Separate authority and restoration

The candidate extends the daemon's existing native serial owner. It adds no
second device owner, discovery, public HTTP tuning route or automatic motion.

- I operations have their own typed entry, parameter kind, backup confirmation,
  pending journal, terminal proof and cross-process restore-only recovery.
  Profile and P-only operations cannot apply or close an I session.
- Only motor 15 and raw I0/I1 are accepted. The selected two-byte register 82 is
  the only maintenance write. Existing P300/I0 baselines and every other
  protected setting must match before preparation.
- Exact nine-motor preimages are content addressed and reused when identical.
  The existing full rollback archives remain the deployment baseline. Each
  distinct transaction still records its small original preimage and intent;
  the copied preimage hash must be confirmed before Apply or handoff.
- Apply is recorded durably before dispatch and cannot be replayed. A missing
  or invalid acknowledgement blocks another Apply and motion handoff. Explicit
  restoration uses fresh reads; an already restored I0 is not written again.
- Disabled preparation cannot grant motion. Awake preparation requires a fresh,
  stationary position hold, the existing identity/version/ownership gates and
  inactive tracking, speech offsets and gravity compensation.
- Awake handoff retains the same worker, command generation, cached-feedback
  freshness checks and six-head publication path. Body and antennas stay held.
  The first comparison target rechecks the measured start after polling resumes.
- A fault revokes output and retains recovery access. Exact original closure
  and, after a feedback fault, a separate fresh-cache acknowledgement are needed
  to release ordinary control. Restoration retains the previously qualified
  one-count excluded-encoder allowance only after journaled head motion.

There is **no firmware I-exposure watchdog** in this candidate. Four permitted
legs and a finite planned hold do not bound elapsed exposure after a stalled
client or lost process. Revoking target output does not restore I0. No automatic
restore, torque change or uncertain write replay is introduced. A later hardware
run needs an available attended operator and a qualified recovery path; if that
cannot be maintained, it must remain disabled-only.

## Prepared comparison protocol

Use A1 I0, B motor-15 I1, A2 restored I0. Keep P300 and all other settings fixed.
Use the same frozen six-axis head pose and held body reference in every condition.
Do not combine these scores with the earlier P360 experiment.

1. Qualify the new ARM native extension and daemon modules in isolated PTYs,
   including acknowledgement loss, interrupted output and cross-process recovery.
   Then qualify I0/I1/I0 with motors disabled and confirm protected readbacks.
2. For each moving condition, use original settings for Wake, require the visual
   raised/settled check, and repeat the original-P300 protocol-2 common start.
   Its +10 mm staging and return are unscored and must close at original I0/P300.
3. Verify the unchanged 1 mm / 1° combined start criterion. Prepare the separate
   I transaction, copy/confirm the exact preimage, apply I1 only in B, and verify
   readback. After handoff, wait read-only for a fresh sample whose timestamp
   advances beyond the pre-handoff cache watermark, then check the start again.
   Keep the same frozen reference; never rebase it to an endpoint residual.
4. Run exactly **−24 mm, neutral, +10 mm, neutral**, relative to that frozen full
   pose, with two-second ramps and ten-second observation holds. The daemon
   accepts these four targets in order, refuses a fifth, and permits no other
   I-comparison motion. Review each actual measured-start path before submission.
5. Seal/export each trace after movement. Capture all joints' delivered goals,
   servo goal/trajectory/present registers, PWM/current, voltage, temperature,
   hardware errors and feedback timing. Score the selected joint's error over
   the hold, together with all-joint error and FK tilt. Job completion alone
   does not establish arrival. Pollen's retained sampled player need not emit
   the mathematical endpoint, so compare actual delivered/read-back counts too.
6. Reclaim and restore I0 explicitly. Independently verify original settings,
   resumed fresh feedback and post-restoration behavior before original-settings
   Sleep. Use the planned Sleep/Wake between conditions and leave it asleep.

Pause at new oscillation, increasing overshoot/drift, unusual effort or heating,
contact/strain, error bits, stale feedback, lost ownership or uncertain completion.
Preserve the first fault and do not submit the next target. Revoke, inspect and
explicitly restore originals through the qualified path; do not replay a target
or assume an inhibited controller has already reset the gain. Repeated exposure
or longer holds require review rather than automatic escalation.

Interpret a small or absent change cautiously: I1 may be too weak over this
observation period, while other joints, hysteresis and hidden servo behavior
remain. A reversible decrease in error over time at identical goals would support
an integral-response component. It would not establish a complete cause or a
permanent tuning choice.

## Offline evidence and remaining gates

Host tests exercise the actual compiled native worker and Python bindings with
a register-bus emulator, plus the retained Pollen head trajectory and daemon
guard with synthetic IK/FK. They cover exact write addresses, all other protected
bytes, backup confirmation, parameter-kind separation, failed or uncertain
writes, generation revocation, four-target scope, feedback faults, closure
reconciliation and restore-only recovery. Existing P/profile/observer and
communication regressions are retained.

These fixtures do not simulate motor torque, gravity, friction, the firmware
integrator or physical stability. The dated [status](STATUS.md) records test
counts and qualification stages. Private source pins, package hashes, failed
attempts, fixture receipts and the prepared protocol remain in ignored storage.
No new full robot backup, mode correction, deployment or publication is included.
